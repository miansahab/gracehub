1. Executive Summary
The Detroit Public Schools Community District Foundation has defined a clear ambition for
GRACE Hub: to establish a single operating platform across fundraising, grants, finance,
communications, programs, events, board activities, and internal operations.
Today, these functions are supported through separate processes, systems, documents, and
spreadsheets. This limits cross-department visibility, increases administrative effort, and makes
organization-wide reporting more difficult.
Clustox proposes a six-month implementation of GRACE Hub covering the Foundation’s Phase
1, Phase 2, and Phase 3 requirements.
The proposed platform is designed to deliver three outcomes:
A unified organizational record
Donor, grant, financial, program, event, board, and operational information will be managed
through a shared data and permission model.
More consistent execution
Configurable workflows, task management, notifications, approvals, and reporting will reduce
manual coordination across departments.
Stronger management visibility
Leadership will have access to consolidated dashboards, pending approvals, financial and
fundraising performance, grant activity, and community-impact reporting.
The platform will be delivered through six structured releases, allowing the Foundation to review
progress, validate requirements, and manage implementation risk throughout the engagement.
Phase 4 capabilities, including multi-organization tenancy, white-labeling, partner portals,
subscription management, public APIs, and a module marketplace, are outside the current
scope. The proposed architecture will, however, avoid design decisions that would prevent
these capabilities from being introduced later.

2. Our Understanding of GRACE Hub
GRACE Hub is intended to become the Foundation’s shared operating system rather than
another standalone departmental application.
The platform must:
● centralize information across departments;
● reduce dependence on disconnected spreadsheets and tools;
● automate approvals, reminders, reviews, and handoffs;
● provide role-specific dashboards and reporting;
● support donor, grant, finance, event, program, board, and operational workflows;
● integrate with the Foundation’s Microsoft and financial systems;
● centralize documents, policies, templates, and organizational knowledge;
● introduce AI capabilities with appropriate governance and human oversight; and
● maintain requirement-level traceability through development, testing, and acceptance.
The Foundation’s technical specification assigns unique identifiers to individual requirements.
Clustox will use these identifiers throughout design, delivery, testing, and acceptance.

● server-side authorization;
● encryption in transit and at rest;
● secure secrets management;
● immutable audit logging;
● separated development, staging, and production environments; and
● backup and disaster-recovery controls.

● letter and communication drafting;
● predictive dashboards;
● automated reporting;
● workflow intelligence; and
● executive insights.
Foundation data will not be used to train public third-party models. AI-generated content will
remain in draft form until reviewed by an authorized user. Knowledge-assistant responses will
cite their supporting source documents, and AI activity will be recorded in the audit history.
Integrations
The platform will integrate with:
● Azure Entra ID for identity and provisioning;
● Microsoft 365 for email, calendars, documents, and meeting artifacts;
● QuickBooks for accounting and financial data; and
● Bill.com for vendors, bills, approvals, and payment status.
Integration processes will include secure credentials, queues, retries, reconciliation,
administrator-visible logs, and error alerts.

8. Testing and Acceptance
Clustox will maintain a requirements traceability matrix linking each requirement ID to the
relevant design item, development task, test case, and acceptance evidence.
Testing will include:
● unit and functional testing;
● integration and failure-mode testing;
● permission and security testing;
● migration validation;
● accessibility and browser testing;
● performance testing;
● regression testing; and
● user acceptance testing.
A staging environment with representative anonymized data will be made available for UAT.
Acceptance will require all applicable mandatory requirements to pass, no unresolved critical or
high-severity defects, completion of the relevant security review, and sign-off by the
Foundation’s project owner.

11. Assumptions and Exclusions
Assumptions
● The supplied technical specification represents the current scope.
● The Foundation will provide access to Azure Entra ID, Microsoft 365, QuickBooks,
Bill.com, and relevant documentation.
● Required third-party accounts, licenses, and sandbox environments will be available
when needed.
● Existing source data will be provided in accessible formats.
● The Foundation will support data mapping, cleansing decisions, and migration validation.
● Feedback and approvals will be provided within agreed review periods.
● The initial implementation will be single-tenant.
● The solution will be delivered as a responsive web platform.
● Final hosting, AI provider, data migration, email infrastructure, and disaster-recovery
decisions will be confirmed during design review.
Exclusions
The proposal does not include:
● Phase 4 enterprise expansion;
● native mobile applications;
● undocumented third-party integrations;
● extensive manual data entry or data reconstruction;
● third-party software licenses;
● Azure hosting charges;
● Microsoft licensing;
● QuickBooks or Bill.com licensing;
● AI model usage charges;
● email, SMS, OCR, or payment-provider charges;
